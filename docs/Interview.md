# **Interview Notes**

## 1. K. Sai – Manager, Siddhartha Voluntary Blood Bank
### Key Learnings

| **Question** | **Answer** |
|---|---|
| **Q1. How do you currently manage and track the availability of different blood groups in your blood bank?** | Honestly, we maintain stock registers. Whenever blood is collected or issued, we update the details so that we know how much stock is available for each blood group. |
| **Q2. What are the biggest challenges you face when there is a shortage of a particular blood group?** | The main problem is finding compatible donors quickly. Especially with rare blood groups, it becomes difficult because we may not have suitable voluntary donors available at that moment. |
| **Q3. How do you receive and respond to urgent blood requests from hospitals?** | Usually, hospitals contact us through phone calls and tell us what blood group and how many units they need. We check our stock and inform them whether we can provide it or need some more time. |
| **Q4. How do you coordinate with other blood banks when the required blood group is unavailable?** | We generally call nearby blood banks or contact them through WhatsApp. We ask whether they have the required blood group and then coordinate with them to arrange the blood if it is available. |
| **Q5. Would a centralized platform connecting hospitals, blood banks and donors be useful?** | Yes, definitely. Honestly, it would make our work much easier because we could check availability and receive requests in one place instead of contacting different blood banks individually. |
| **Q6. What is the one feature you would expect from this website?** | Honestly, I would expect a live blood stock availability feature. It should show how many units of each blood group are available in different blood banks so that we can quickly find stock during emergencies. |
| **Q7. What would make you hesitate to use an online platform for blood requests?** | Honestly, my main concern would be incorrect blood stock information. If the website shows that blood is available but we actually don't have it, it could create confusion during emergencies. |

## 2. N. Anand – Vasundhara Blood Bank

### Key Learnings

| **Question** | **Answer** |
|---|---|
| **Q1. How do you currently manage and track the availability of different blood groups in your blood bank?** | We maintain records of blood collection, issued units and the remaining stock. We also keep checking expiry dates because it is important to know which units are available and safe to use. |
| **Q2. What are the biggest challenges you face when there is a shortage of a particular blood group?** | Honestly, maintaining enough stock is sometimes difficult, especially when fewer people come forward to donate. If updating the details takes too much time or the system is complicated to use, it might become difficult for us to manage it regularly. |

## Overall Findings

The interviews highlighted the following common challenges:

**1.Difficulty finding the right contact**: Hospitals and blood banks may need to contact multiple people or organizations to arrange the required blood.

**2.Scattered communication**: Phone calls and WhatsApp groups are commonly used, but messages may not reach the right person or receive an immediate response.

**3.Availability confirmation**: Blood banks need reliable information about available stock before responding to requests.

**4.Coordination delays**: When blood is unavailable locally, contacting other blood banks and confirming availability can take additional time.

**5.Need for organized communication**: Interviewees expressed interest in a platform that helps them search, send requests, receive updates and coordinate blood requirements.

These findings support the problem of difficulty in finding the right blood-related contact and coordinating urgent blood requirements.
